
- **Broker:** An independent entity that helps customers select appropriate insurance coverage and companies. They represent the customer, cannot directly bind coverage, and may charge client fees in addition to earning commissions.

- **Insurance Agent:** Represents the insurance company to sell policies to customers and build ongoing client relationships. Unlike brokers, agents have the authority to bind coverage on behalf of the insurer and work solely on commission from the insurer.

- **Insurance Carrier (Insurer):** The core insurance company responsible for the overall insurance operation, including product development, underwriting, policy administration, claims management, and financial risk management.
- **Managing General Agent (MGA):** A specialized type of agent with broader responsibilities. Functioning like a small insurance company operating in a niche, an MGA can develop products, perform underwriting, and manage claims, but relies on a larger insurer for financial backing.

- **Reinsurer:** A company that provides financial protection to insurance companies by accepting a portion of their risk portfolio in exchange for a premium or fee.

- **Capacity Provider:** Similar to a reinsurer, but offers a broader financial commitment by injecting additional capital to financially back an insurer or MGA, enabling them to underwrite and sign more policies.

- **Underwriter:** Internal staff responsible for assessing and managing risk to determine whether to accept policy applications and under what terms.

- **Claims Adjuster / Handler / Assessor:** Individuals responsible for evaluating, verifying, and processing claims, preventing fraud, and determining appropriate payout amounts.
- **Actuary:** Professionals responsible for analyzing data, designing products, and creating pricing tables.

- **Policyholder:** The customer who purchases the insurance policy, serving as the ultimate source of income for all parties in the value chain.
